# Stage 2 — Core Skills Lab: learning standard and lesson-by-lesson audit

Last review: 15 October 2026. **Status: stronger learning tasks deployed; proficiency outcomes NOT validated.**
Not an IELTS/CEFR certificate. Completion and correctness are separate concepts.

## Purpose
Stage 2 converts a learner's A0–A2 language knowledge into effective task behaviour before high-stakes exam formats: read for evidence, follow natural speech, form accurate sentences, develop explanations, and interact spontaneously. Stage 3 must never substitute for these foundations.

## Required assessment pillars
1. **Meaning:** identify the actual meaning, relationships, time and scope.
2. **Recognition:** answer questions on a new example without seeing explanations.
3. **Production:** generate a genuine written or spoken response.
4. **Interaction:** respond to corrections, changes or unexpected follow-ups.
5. **Feedback and repair:** explain one misconception, correct it and retry.
6. **Retention/transfer:** demonstrate use in a distinct context after a delay.

## Ten lesson acceptance criteria
1. One observable can-do action linked to a Stage 1 prerequisite.
2. A clear communicative context and intended audience.
3. Explanation plus a meaning-changing counterexample.
4. Guided practice with explicit reasoning and accessible difficulty.
5. **An unfamiliar evidence check** that does not repeat the taught example.
6. A new production task where the learner has to think, not copy the sample.
7. **Check** evaluates task compliance and meaning first, quotes actual errors and gives one high-priority revision.
8. At least one successful second attempt, transfer to another prompt, and a delayed review.
9. Listening and Speaking must be validated with **real audio**, not typed notes, browser TTS or self-checkboxes alone.
10. A lesson is not marked mastered solely due to 2/2 MCQ, opening a sample, typing a sentence, or viewing a feedback screen.

## Skill-specific requirements
| Domain | Observable capability | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking | Parse grammar roles, explain reasoning, reject unsupported inferences, preserve meaning across paraphrases | Fresh claim/evidence tasks, self-generated examples and explanations |
| Listening | Decode sound boundaries, gist, exact details, changes, speaker opinions, lecture structure | New real audio, transcript AFTER check, and specific error replay |
| Reading | Identify main idea, scan, resolve pronouns, TFNG/NG, paraphrase and inference | New written passage, location of support and rejection of assumptions |
| Writing | Produce accurate sentences, topic paragraphs, logical support, appropriate references and revised drafts | Own first draft, specific correction and revised second draft |
| Speaking | Answer first, develop reasons/examples, describe, narrate, paraphrase and use intelligible stress | Actual audio, spontaneous follow-up and feedback tied to audible features |

## What is actually in the repo
- Existing 30 Core Skills units (6 per area), 60 original multiple-choice questions.
- New 30 distinct contextual questions and 30 independent practice/transfer prompts in Stage 2 Evidence.
- Optional authenticated **Check** for typed practice, saved correction and revision note. AI feedback is practice, not an IELTS band.
- Former quiz-only repeated-day mastery is tightened. For Listening and Speaking, typed feedback is explicitly NOT proof of listening or spoken delivery.
- Prior records under `gk_core_skills_v1` are preserved. New evidence under `gk_stage2_evidence_v1`.

## Outstanding acceptance blockers
- Most original lessons still have two recognition items; independent tasks need more unseen forms and progressive difficulty.
- Stage 2 Listening currently has synthetic and text-based examples; multiple natural recordings and audio-only comprehension are required.
- Stage 2 Speaking currently prompts production, but does not yet score two-way spoken interaction or pronunciation from actual audio.
- Real learner trials, consistency of AI feedback, retesting after days, phone/microphone usability and error-driven routing have not yet been validated.
- A progress or mastery label is NOT a CEFR or IELTS certification.

## Next actual acceptance test
For each unit, ask a learner to explain a misconception, attempt a new task without help, correct their error, and perform an unfamiliar delayed variant after 3+ days. Collect listening audio evidence and spontaneous speaking follow-up. Quality gate passes only when those outputs have been verified; unit count alone is insufficient.
